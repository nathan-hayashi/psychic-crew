We walk the dogs and sit with the cats while you are out.

Kettle Lane Cats & Dogs is two sitters covering one street and the four either side of it. We are close enough to walk to your door, which is the whole point: a midday visit should not depend on anyone finding parking.

Every visit ends with a short written note — what your animal ate, where you went, and anything that looked different from last time. The notes are for you, not for a feed.

What a visit is

Two kinds of visit, priced the same, because a cat who wants forty minutes of company is not less work than a dog who wants forty minutes of pavement.

Show visits for

  • The midday walk

    Forty minutes, on the lead, on routes we already know. Long grass in the summer, the gritted side of the road in the winter. Towel-dry and fresh water before we leave.

  • The two-dog round

    Housemates walk together at no extra charge. Dogs from different homes only walk together after they have met on neutral ground with both owners present.

  • The drop-in

    Thirty minutes: food, water, tray, and then we sit down and let the cat decide what happens next. Shy cats get a chair and a quiet room, not a search of the house.

  • Medication and older cats

    Tablets, ear drops, and subcutaneous fluids if your vet has shown us the routine first. We write down the time each dose was given, every single time.

  • The holiday week

    Twice a day while you are away, plus post, plants, and bins. One sitter takes the whole week so your animal sees the same face each morning.

How a day actually runs

  1. Half past seven. Keys come off the board in the order of the round. Two keys are never on the same ring, and none of them carries your address.
  2. Nine to two. The walks and drop-ins, in the same order each day. Animals notice the order. Ours have opinions about it.
  3. Before we leave a house. Water changed, tray checked, door tried twice from the outside. The note goes through your letterbox or onto the kitchen table.
  4. Half past five. Keys back on the board, and anything that looked off gets a phone call rather than a note — a limp, a full bowl, a tray that stayed clean.

Who turns up

Marta spent eleven years as a veterinary nurse and still cannot walk past a limp without commenting on it. She takes the medication rounds and the older animals.

Ade came to this from twenty years of shift work and likes that the round starts at the same time every day. He takes the long walks and the nervous dogs.

Between us we are insured, first-aid trained for animals, and known to every one of the three vets within walking distance. There is no third sitter, and there will not be one until we can no longer answer the door ourselves.

Booking

Booking starts with a visit that is not a visit: half an hour at your kitchen table with your animal in the room, so nobody's first meeting happens through a door.

  1. Write to us and say which street you are on and who lives with you.
  2. We come round, meet the animal, and write down the routine you already use.
  3. If everyone is happy, we take a key and start on the day you name.

Write to hello@kettlelane.example, or leave a note at number 14 — the green door with the boot scraper. We answer once in the morning and once in the evening, which is slower than a message app and about as fast as anything needs to be.
